# Checkpoint 4: Aircraft DME with the Course Ground Station IP (final project)

The final-project setup. The aircraft DME and the course ground station IP
(`groundStation`, Vivado 2022.2) run in the same FPGA and are connected
directly, with no ADC or DAC. It covers all six project requirements:

| | Requirement | How |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Distance, static | reply delay → distance, averaged over 1 s |
| 2 | Distance and speed, towards / away | speed from the timing of the station's 1 km steps |
| 3 | As 2, 60% reply efficiency | histogram peak needs only 15% of interrogations answered |
| 4 | Distance, static, squitter up to 2500 pps | random PRF jitter + delay histogram: squitter spreads out, replies do not |
| 5 | As 2, with squitter | as 4 |
| 6 | As 3, with squitter | as 4 |

**To build and run it on the ZedBoard, follow [BUILD.md](BUILD.md)**: one
Vivado script (bitstream and `.xsa`), one XSCT script (`.elf`), one to
program the board.

```text
                 ┌──────────────────────── dme_aircraft_axi4lite ──────────────────────────┐
  PS (AXI) ────► │ interrogator TX ──tx_sample (12-bit)──┐                                 │
                 │  7 us half-sine, 12 us apart, 10 MHz  │      reply FIFO (delay, time)    │
                 │                                       │              ▲                   │
                 │ envelope demod ─► pair detector ──────┼──────────────┘                   │
                 └────────▲──────────────────────────────┼──────────────────────────────────┘
                          │ rx_sample (12-bit)           ▼ i_dme_data
                 ┌────────┴──────────── groundStation (course IP, unmodified) ──────────────┐
                 │ demodulator ─► efficiency ─► delay (distance) ─► 50 us reply ─► delay ─► │
                 │                                                    10 MHz carrier mixer  │
                 └──────────────────────────▲───────────────────────────────────────────────┘
  PS (AXI) ────► dme_gs_ctrl_axi4lite ──────┘ distance, speed, direction, efficiency, ...
```

## What changed from checkpoint 3

The course ground station replaces our checkpoint 3 station, and its format
is fixed:

| | Checkpoints 1 to 3 | Course IP (now) |
|---|---|---|
| Pulse | 50 us raised cosine | 7 us half-sine (700 samples, peak 2047) |
| Pair spacing | 400 us | 12 us, rising edge to rising edge |
| Carrier | 1 MHz | 10 MHz |
| Samples | 16-bit signed | 12-bit signed |

- **Transmitter** (`rtl/dme_interrogator_tx.sv`, new). The checkpoint 1
  modulator times pulses in whole microseconds and uses an external 1 MHz
  DDS. The new one counts clock cycles. It reads the pulse shape from a
  700-entry ROM (`dme_halfsine_rom.sv`, generated by
  `sim/gen_halfsine_rom.py`). The 10 MHz carrier is a 10-entry cosine table,
  exact at 100 MHz, so no DDS is needed. The product is divided by 2048
  like the station's own mixer. The pulse shape matters: the station's
  reply generator only accepts pulses its demodulator sees as 6.5 to 7.5
  us wide, with a 4.5 to 5.5 us gap. A flat-topped 7 us pulse fails,
  because the peak detector's decay tail (10 per clock) stretches it. The
  half-sine is detected as 6.63 us with a 5.37 us gap.
- **PRF jitter.** Each interrogation period gets a random 0 to
  `TX_JITTER` extra cycles (software: up to 0.65 ms). Squitter and other
  replies not caused by our interrogations then land at random delays,
  while our replies keep one delay. See [Squitter](#squitter).
- **Receiver.** The checkpoint 2 `dme_envelope_demodulator` and
  `dme_pulse_pair_detector` are reused unchanged, with new settings:
  IIR shift 4 (16-cycle, 0.16 us time constant), thresholds 10000/8000 (envelope peak
  is about 21800, so this is the half-amplitude point), width 4 to 6.5 us,
  spacing 11.5 to 12.5 us.
- **Ranging.** Each reply pair goes into a 64-entry FIFO, together with
  its delay in cycles from the start of the interrogation it answers
  (as in checkpoint 3). A range gate (`MAX_DELAY`, default 270000 cycles,
  just beyond 389 km) keeps pairs that arrive later out of the FIFO, so
  squitter between interrogations cannot fill it.
- **Ground station control** (`rtl/dme_gs_ctrl_axi4lite.sv`, new). It
  drives all of the IP's inputs from software, so every test case runs
  without a rebuild.

## Distance

```text
delay      = interrogation start → first reply pulse at half amplitude (cycles)
           = K + 2 × floor(d × 334 × 33 / 32768)            (station's delayModel.v)
distance   = (delay − K) × 32768 / (334 × 33) / 2   meters
           = (delay − K) × 1.48648 m
```

- **K** is the delay at zero distance: the station's fixed 50 us
  (`replyGen` `fixedDelay = 5000`, from the rising edge of the first
  pulse) plus every pipeline latency. In simulation it is 5168 cycles
  (51.68 us). The software measures it at start-up with the station at
  10 km. It does not use 0 km, because at zero range the station's delay
  FIFO starts reading 2 cycles earlier than at any other distance.
- **Scale.** The station delays by 3.364 us/km one way, slightly more than
  the free-space 3.336 us/km. The software uses the station's scale so the
  displayed distance matches the distance set in it. With the free-space
  value it would read 0.84% short.
- **Resolution and accuracy.** One cycle of delay is 1.49 m. In simulation
  each reply is within ±7 cycles (±10 m) of the model, from the 10 MHz
  carrier phase at the start of each pulse. The software averages about 30
  replies per second.
- **Range limit.** The station works up to about 389 km (210 NM). Its delay
  FIFO is 131072 cycles deep, and `i_dist_m × 334 × 33` overflows 32 bits
  just beyond that. The control IP's driver clamps distances to 389000 m.

## Squitter

How the course IP makes squitter (`squitterGen.v`, `pulseMux.v`,
`replyGen.v`):

- One pair every `1e8 / i_pulse_rate + 1` cycles (40001 at 2500 pps), at a
  fixed phase. It goes through the same distance delay as replies.
- The output mux sends one source at a time. A reply that falls while a
  squitter pair is being sent (about 6% of the time at 2500 pps) is lost.
  After each reply the station sends nothing for `1e8 / i_pulse_rate`
  cycles.

Each second the software (`software/dme_track.c`) builds a histogram of all
reply delays (256-cycle bins). Our interrogations are randomly jittered by
up to 65535 cycles, more than one squitter period, so squitter delays are
spread evenly: at 2500 pps, about 6.7 squitter pairs per interrogation over
1050 bins. Our replies all fall in one bin. The strongest pair of adjacent
bins is the reply. Its median, then the mean of the replies within ±32
cycles of it (±160 with the station's reply jitter on), gives the distance.
At 60% efficiency and 2500 pps the peak has about 17 replies per second,
against well under 1 squitter pair in any one bin.

## Speed

The course IP's `distanceModel.v` moves the distance in **1 km steps**,
one every `floor(1000 / i_speed)` seconds. A requested 150 m/s is really
1 km per 6 s, or 166.7 m/s. The tracker:

1. Notices a step when the block's delay changes by more than 3 gates.
2. Times it to within one interrogation period (33 ms). It splits the
   replies of this and the previous second, in time order, into "old" and
   "new" distance at the point with the fewest replies on the wrong side.
3. Speed is (distance at the last step − distance at the first) / (time
   between them), over up to 8 steps in the same direction. After two
   steps (10 s at 200 m/s) it is exact to about 0.1%. It is also limited to
   1 km / (time since the last step), so it falls when the motion stops.
4. Before two steps, or when steps stop, speed is the least-squares slope
   of the last 16 one-second distances (shown as `(fit)`).

Speeds below 47 m/s do not work in the IP: `countPerKM = 100000000 ×
seconds` overflows a 32-bit integer.

## Simulation

```sh
./checkpoint4/sim/run_sim.sh
```

Needs Icarus Verilog 12, about 7 minutes. It simulates the **unmodified**
course IP sources from `ip/groundStation`, with behavioural models of its
four Xilinx cores in `sim/models`. The testbench (`rtl/tb_dme_ckpt4.sv`)
checks:

1. Format: the station accepts every interrogation (663-cycle pulses, 537
   gap, no width/delay errors) and the aircraft decodes every reply.
2. Zero range: delay 5166 cycles for every reply.
3. Static 1 to 389 km: every distance within 12 m.
4. 60% efficiency: 30 of 50 answered, all decoded, all correct.
5. Squitter 2500 pps, static at 10 km: 38 squitter pairs sent, the range
   gate and FIFO work. The tracker replay of the recorded FIFO gives
   9995 m.
6. Moving towards (station motion forced 500x faster: 1 km per 10 ms), 60%
   efficiency, squitter: 10 → 6 km. The tracker replay follows every step
   and gives the speed from step timing within 3% (102363 vs 99999 m/s in
   compressed time) and 5998 m at the end.

In simulation the interrogation period is short (0.49 ms). After every
reply the station sends nothing for 0.4 ms, so fewer squitter pairs get
out than on the board (30 interrogations/s).

One simulation-only issue in the course IP: `ppGen.v` reads `pulseBRAM` one
past its 701 entries at the end of each reply pulse. That sample is X in
simulation, so the testbench passes X to the aircraft as 0.

## Vivado

`vivado/build_project.tcl` builds everything in one run (see
[BUILD.md](BUILD.md)). It works with Vivado 2020.1 and newer. The course
IP's `.xci` files need 2022.2, so the script re-creates its four Xilinx
cores with the original settings and checks them.

```text
design_1 (block design)                         system_top.v (generated)
  ZYNQ7 PS ── M_AXI_GP0 ── AXI interconnect ──► M_AXI_AC  ──► dme_system.ac_*   (0x43C00000)
  FCLK_CLK0 100 MHz, proc_sys_reset         └─► M_AXI_GSC ──► dme_system.gsc_*  (0x43C10000)

dme_system.sv: dme_aircraft_axi4lite + dme_gs_ctrl_axi4lite + groundStation (mod = 0)
```

The course IP and our cores are plain RTL under one top level, not packaged
IP: nothing has to be packaged or repackaged, and SystemVerilog is fine.
The signals between the aircraft and the station are marked `mark_debug`
for an ILA (Tools > Set Up Debug after synthesis).

## Software

`software/main.c` with `dme_track.c` (tracker), `dme_aircraft_driver.c`
and `dme_gs_ctrl_driver.c`. `vitis/build_app.tcl` builds it and
`vitis/run_on_board.tcl` runs it. Over the UART at 115200 baud it:

1. Calibrates the zero-range delay K with the station at 10 km, using the
   histogram peak, so squitter does not disturb it.
2. Puts the station at 100 km, static, and interrogates at 30 pairs/s with
   up to 0.65 ms of random PRF jitter.
3. Prints every second:

   ```text
   t=12s  distance 146998.8 m = 79.373 NM  station 147000 m  error -1.2 m  replies 17/30  other pairs 171  speed 200.0 m/s towards (3 steps)
   ```

   `replies` is the size of the histogram peak against interrogations
   sent, and `other pairs` is the squitter (and anything else) received.
   Speed shows `(n steps)` from step timing, or `(fit)` from the slope.

Commands (end with Enter):

| Command | Effect |
|---|---|
| `1` ... `6` | set up the test case of requirement 1 to 6 |
| `d 50000`, `k 50`, `n 27` | station distance in meters, km or NM (stops the motion) |
| `v 200`, `a`, `t`, `m 1` | speed (m/s), away, towards, motion on |
| `e 60` | reply efficiency in percent |
| `q 1`, `r 2500` | squitter on, squitter rate (pairs/s) |
| `j 1` | station reply jitter (recalibrates) |
| `c` | recalibrate K |
| `s` | aircraft and station counters |

## Tests without hardware

- `software/test/run_tests.sh` (1 s): the tracker against a model of the
  course IP's behaviour (delay model, 1 km steps, efficiency, periodic
  squitter with the mux collisions, reply jitter). 14 scenarios covering
  all six requirements, both directions, the station limit, reply jitter,
  and the motion stopping. Each checks the worst distance error over every
  second, and the speed.
- `sim/run_sim.sh` (about 7 min): the RTL testbench, then the tracker on the
  replies recorded in simulation (below).

## Known limits

- **Not yet run on hardware.** The build scripts were written without a
  Vivado installation; BUILD.md lists what to check in their output.
- The course IP's motion is a staircase. The reported distance follows the
  station's distance exactly, and the speed is the station's real average
  speed, 1000 / floor(1000 / v) m/s.
- With the station's reply jitter on (`j 1`), each reply varies by up to
  255 cycles (380 m). The one-second average then varies by about ±30 m.
- Speeds below 47 m/s: the IP's `distanceModel.v` overflows.
